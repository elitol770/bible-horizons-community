# Reviewing a scene contribution

BibleHorizons welcomes proposed scenes, assets and interaction improvements. This public kit is a place to prepare and review contributions. The official website and its source remain private and owner-controlled. A contribution, a merged kit example or a successful test is not permission to integrate or publish it on the official website. The owner makes that decision after review.

## Define what you are submitting

Start with a short brief: the problem, intended audience, relevant passage, proposed change and what remains outside scope. Choose one scope:

| Scope | What the submission can establish | What it cannot establish by itself |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Asset study | A tree, plant, object or figure can be inspected and used under its documented source terms. | That it fits a chapter, represents a historically identified subject or meets the website's delivery requirements. |
| Interaction study | A bounded navigation or accessibility behavior works in the supplied example. | That all chapters, languages or website recovery paths work. |
| Passage scene | Specific subjects and actions are represented with clear passage bindings. | Complete coverage of the surrounding chapter. |
| Chapter experience | Every passage in the declared edition has an explicit treatment and remains available to read. | Historical certainty, human editorial approval or publication readiness without review. |

For passage and chapter work, include a coverage table: passage range, subject or action, proposed treatment, reader access and known limitations. A treatment may be a scene, an accessible explanation or a reader-only section. Name it honestly. A complete reader does not make an incomplete visual sequence a complete chapter experience.

## Keep evidence and illustration distinct

Identify the Bible edition and the exact passage references. Do not invent, paraphrase or silently alter text presented as a Scripture quotation. If a text correction is needed, report it separately with a verifiable source. Keep commentary and artistic descriptions visibly distinct from quoted text.

For each substantial visual claim, ask which category it belongs to:

- **Textual statement:** a subject, action or relationship stated in the cited passage.
- **Contextual claim:** information from another identifiable source, with its uncertainty and relevance explained.
- **Artistic choice:** appearance, species, terrain, clothing cut, gesture, timing, camera or lighting chosen to make the scene intelligible.

A convincing render is not evidence that an artistic choice is historically correct. Do not label a modern plant as an identified biblical species, an illustrative landscape as a discovered location, or an inferred gesture as a narrated event. Where traditions or editions differ materially, state the scope rather than presenting one interpretation as undisputed.

## Bible and story review

A reviewer should check the following against the declared passage and edition:

- Names, speakers, sequence, relationships and selected verse ranges are accurate. A character's statement is not silently turned into the narrator's assertion.
- Important subjects and actions have a readable treatment. Scenery does not substitute for an event that the submission claims to depict.
- Added details are qualified where they affect interpretation. Cross-chapter imagery is not imported as though it appears in the selected passage.
- Sensitive events are represented with restraint and context. Adult figures, vulnerability, harm and shame are not used as spectacle or as an excuse to obscure the text.
- Reader access, notes and relevant uncertainty remain discoverable. Shorter captions do not erase essential meaning or attribution.
- The coverage table distinguishes completed, partial, reader-only and missing treatments. No unreviewed section is marked complete.

For a standalone asset, keep this review proportional: check the proposed role and claims. A plant mesh does not require a full chapter rewrite, but it must not be submitted as proof of Eden's botany.

## Visual and interaction review

Review the actual submitted artifact, not only a concept image, service thumbnail or authoring screenshot.

- Inspect a 3D asset from front, side, back and below where relevant. Check its full silhouette, connected parts, roots or supports, materials and visible texture seams.
- In a scene, inspect the subject at an ordinary desktop size and a narrow viewport. Keep people, gestures, props and selected objects readable without relying on a favorable close-up.
- Check contact and movement: feet and objects are supported, hands interact with their actual targets, and moving bodies do not pass through plants or scenery. Inspect the whole relevant motion, including pause, replay and reduced-motion states.
- Preserve distinct identities. Nearby decorative objects must not be mistaken for named subjects or a different narrative action.
- Provide keyboard and readable text equivalents for meaningful interactions. Controls need understandable names, visible focus and a usable return path. Do not rely only on color, hover, sound or precise 3D clicking.
- Show loading and failure honestly. An incomplete scene must not look finished, and a missing 3D asset must not remove access to the passage.
- Report measured delivery size, geometry, textures and tested environments. Distinguish estimates from measurements and desktop viewport emulation from a physical-device test. Use maintainer-agreed budgets rather than assuming spare capacity.

Technical clearance and visual readability are separate questions. A collision-free plant can still hide the action; an attractive still can conceal a broken motion or loading state.

## Evidence to include

Provide enough information for someone else to inspect the same version:

1. A concise brief and the coverage table where applicable.
2. Artifact filenames and version or hashes, source/creator records, permissions information and a description of modifications. Include only material you are entitled to share publicly; do not expose private project assets or reports.
3. Reproduction instructions, tested environment and relevant settings.
4. Original screenshots and, for motion, a short continuous recording. Label concept images, authoring previews and actual rendered results separately.
5. Test results and known failures, including what was not tested. Preserve a meaningful failed comparison when it explains a subsequent correction.

If the artifact changes after review, identify the changed files and obtain review of the affected result. Do not reuse an approval for different bytes or claim a new render was inspected when only the previous version was seen.

## Record a bounded decision

Ask for Bible/story review and visual/accessibility review appropriate to the contribution. An author can document checks but should not describe their own assessment as independent review. AI assistance must not be presented as a named human expert's signoff.

A useful review record states the version, scope, evidence inspected, findings, unresolved blockers and decision: **changes required**, **suitable for a further trial**, or **recommended for owner consideration**. Keep source eligibility, technical checks, visual quality and editorial judgment distinct. None of these labels alone means the official website has accepted or published the contribution.
