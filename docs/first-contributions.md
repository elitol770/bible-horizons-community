# First contributions

Choose one bounded study that another person can reproduce and review. These briefs are invitations to propose work, not assignments to change the official website. Use a standalone example with original or publicly shareable materials. Do not obtain or copy the private website's code, models or internal review documents.

Read [the editorial review guide](editorial-review.md) before beginning. State your scope and assumptions early. The maintainer may request a smaller study or a different direction before integration is considered.

## 1. Eden: a convincing low groundcover patch

**Problem:** a garden floor can look like a flat color field or a collection of repeated upright cards. Create one small, coherent patch with visible rooted structure that remains useful at ordinary viewing distance.

Build a low, irregular arrangement of thin leaves and stems, with overlapping growth, modest gaps and a broken edge. Show it on a simple sloped ground surface using neutral lighting. Record actual dimensions, full footprint, geometry, textures and any motion. Use a scale reference without requiring a human model.

Keep the proposal artistic. A botanical source name belongs in its provenance; it does not identify the plants of Eden. Do not add fruit, harvesting, a growth event or a new reading of Genesis 2. This is an asset and floor-composition study, not a complete garden or chapter.

Submit:

- The complete patch and its source/modification record.
- Front, side and oblique views showing root support and leaf thickness, plus a narrow-viewport view at a declared distance.
- A simple footprint and maximum-height diagram, including any wind excursion.
- A brief account of repetition, intersections, delivery cost and remaining defects.

A useful result has readable depth without becoming a hedge, floating sheet or obvious repeated stamp. Actual actor and prop clearance must be checked separately if the patch is later proposed for a story scene. Asset approval alone would not authorize that placement.

## 2. Four rivers: clearer explanation or layout

**Problem:** a river presentation should help readers distinguish the shared river description from the four named courses without presenting an illustration as a verified map.

Propose a small diagram, explanatory interaction or standalone landscape layout for Genesis 2:10–14. Preserve one shared garden river dividing into four distinct named courses. The exact bends, spacing, orientation and fork shape are illustrative choices; do not add a modern geographic pin or claim to resolve Eden's location.

Use these passage bindings, checked against the declared edition:

| Subject | Genesis 2 range |
| --- | --- |
| Shared river and division | 10 |
| Pishon | 11–12 |
| Gihon | 13 |
| Hiddekel | 14 |
| Euphrates | 14 |

Preserve the passage's naming order and textual associations. Do not turn screen order or compass direction into a biblical claim. An overview plus four selectable river views is five controls, not five rivers. If using a perspective landscape, one view may show only part of it; provide a clear way to understand all four courses and their common relationship.

Submit a readable overview, narrow-viewport example and keyboard/text equivalent. Each named selection should identify its exact passage and make returning straightforward. Shared reaches should not receive an exclusive river identity without explanation. Avoid reconnections or extra courses that confuse the proposed one-to-four reading.

A successful study improves understanding and preserves uncertainty. It does not establish historical hydrology, complete Genesis 2 coverage or acceptance of new terrain on the website.

## 3. Accessible scene navigation

**Problem:** readers should understand what is selected, move between passages and reach the text without having to master a 3D interface.

Create a small standalone navigation example using a clearly labeled sample set of passage-linked scenes. Include an explicit statement that the sample is not the full Bible or a complete chapter. Use exact references; a list of references and original interface copy is sufficient without reproducing Bible text.

Keep the selected scene and passage clear. Provide direct selection, understandable previous/next destinations and access to the selected passage or full chapter as appropriate. Avoid several controls with different behavior but the same vague “Read” label. Distinguish an interface language from the Bible edition being read, and distinguish chapters with a scene from chapters available only in the reader.

Test:

- Keyboard selection and activation, visible focus, and closing a dialog back to a meaningful control.
- A narrow viewport, long labels and zoomed text without trapped scrolling or clipped actions.
- Loading, unavailable 3D and failed-asset states with a usable reading path.
- Pause and reduced motion where the example includes animation.
- Selection persistence through Back/Forward or reload if the example claims to support those behaviors.

If using links, preserve normal link behavior, including opening in another tab. If state is represented in a URL, do not claim navigation succeeded when the URL update failed. Report which languages, browsers and assistive technologies were actually checked; do not imply unperformed accessibility certification.

Submit the runnable example, concise reproduction steps, original screenshots and a short account of what became clearer. It is an interaction proposal, not permission to replace the website's navigation or remove its reader, source context or recovery behavior.

## Keep the handoff small and honest

For any brief, explain the concrete improvement, what is included, what remains untested and how the maintainer can review it. A clear rejected or partial result is more useful than a polished claim of completion unsupported by the artifact. All official integration remains subject to review and the owner's decision.
